# Field Validation Conversation Guide — 30 Minutes of Opportunity Validation

The conversation pattern for taking a surviving hypothesis card into the field. **A human does the talking** (signity Tanebi's job ends at handing over this guide). Grounded in Continuous Discovery principles: don't sell, don't open with the solution, ask about behavior.

## Premise: what these 30 minutes are for

**To learn precisely what the person is struggling with (= the behavior they already engage in).** Not to win an order. Whether it sells is a downstream result. Why this purpose must be protected: the moment you utter a solution, the other person stops describing their own behavior and becomes an evaluator of your proposal. The listening session turns into a pitch, and it never turns back.

## Skeleton of the conversation (30 minutes, 4 blocks)

| Min | Block | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Entry | Thank them, and state the purpose of the session in one sentence ("This is not a pitch — I'd like to learn what is eating your time right now") |
| 3–15 | Pin down the behavior | Get one thing, in their own words, that they currently spend time or money on |
| 15–25 | Slice it | Verify volume, who does it, who decides, and the deadline |
| 25–30 | Exit | Set the next single touchpoint. **Do not produce a quote or proposal on the spot** |

## Question patterns (min 3–15: pin down the behavior)

| # | Question | What you are going after |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | "What task eats the most of your time right now?" | The entry point into behavior. Ask for actual work, not impressions |
| Q2 | "How many hours a week (or month) is that? Who does it?" | Volume and who carries it. The only question that reveals scale without talking money |
| Q3 | "If it disappeared, what would you do with that time?" | Value check. A struggle with no answer here brings no joy even when solved |

## Question patterns (min 15–25: slice it)

| # | Question | Branch |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 | "Would you rather learn to do this yourself, or hand it to someone and just use the result?" | Branches the shape of the product (wants to learn / wants to delegate) |
| Q5 | "Are you the one who can decide to go ahead?" | Decision-maker check. If not, the proposal target is someone else |
| Q6 | "Is there a date by which you want this handled?" | Deadline. If there is none, they are not struggling |

## Extra probes from the hypothesis card (when refutation attached "conditions for survival")

- **Behavior existence**: "When did you last do this? How did you do it that time?" — verify with a recent, concrete episode. Agreement with a generality is not evidence
- **Existing means check**: "What do you use now (or whom do you ask)? How much do you pay?" — someone who has already solved it is not this hypothesis's customer (the adverse-selection check)
- **Willingness to pay**: "If it were roughly X, how much per month would you pay? What do you pay today for the same purpose?" — take it as **a concrete amount and a current payment behavior**, never as a stated "I'd love to use it"

## Recording

After the conversation, the same day, fill the card's "Field validation result" section: behaviors confirmed (in the person's own words), what could not be confirmed, verdict (supported / rejected / revised). If the interview confirmed the behavior, that item is promoted from "opportunity hypothesis" to "opportunity" — the exit of this guide is the updated card.
